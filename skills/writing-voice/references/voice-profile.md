# Voice profile

How Tomi sounds in each register, with the phrases he reaches for. The patterns come from his own sent email, product updates, talk scripts, product copy, feedback write-ups, the rules he wrote for his own notes, and the corrections he gave on drafts. Names, companies, and private details are left out on purpose: this repo is public.

The quoted lines are real, lightly generalized. They show a move. Don't reuse a product line as copy for another piece: its claim was true of one product on one day.

Each table row is a slot, and the phrases are examples of what fills it. Use the slots the message needs, one phrase per slot, and write your own wording as often as you borrow his. Imitate the rhythm, never the typos: he drops accents and leaves a typo when he types fast, and a draft in his voice has neither.

## The constants

These hold in every register and both languages.

| Trait | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| Answer, reason, next step | Three short paragraphs in that order. Often three sentences |
| Ownership | He says what he did or will do, with a time: "I have just shared...", "I'll come back on Monday with our plan" |
| A way out for the reader | "If those times don't work...", "si te interesa...", "sin problema" |
| Reason given, not assumed | "For context, ...", "La razón es que..." |
| Calibrated, not absolute | "Completely acceptable, but it's an improvement opportunity", "virtudes y falencias" |
| Credit to the other side | "The team quickly clarified and fixed both cases" |
| Thanks at the edges | "Thanks in advance!", "Gracias desde ya," |
| One light aside | A parenthesis or a single dry joke, never a run of them |

## English: work email and chat

**Shape of a reply:** greeting, one line of reaction, what he did or the answer, the ask or the way out, thanks, sign-off. Four to six short lines.

| Slot | He writes |
|------|-----------|
| Greeting, email | `Hi <Name>,` by default. `Hi <Name>!` when replying to good news |
| Greeting, Slack | `Hey @Name!` to open a DM. `Thanks @Name for ...!` to open a thread reply |
| Reaction | "Great news!" · "Great to hear from you!" · "Thanks for the clarification! That makes perfect sense." · "Sounds great, thanks for the quick response and update!" |
| What he did | "I have just submitted my availability." · "I rescheduled it for Monday." · "Received the invite. Thank you!" |
| Way out | "If those times don't work, please send 2 or 3 possible slots from your end, and I can adjust." · "If it conflicts with your schedule, please let me know, and I will do my best to adjust." |
| Nudge | "I wanted to quickly check if you received ..." · "I'm checking in to see if you have any updates on ..." |
| Asking for a fix | "Could you please resend it?" then "Sorry for the inconvenience, and thanks in advance!" |
| Close | "Thanks in advance!" · "Looking forward to speaking with the team on Thursday." · "Thanks, and I hope you have a great week!" |
| Sign-off | `Best,` or `Best regards,` then `Tomas` by default, `Tomi` with people who already call him that. A second reply in the same exchange on the same day has no greeting and no sign-off |

**Several questions at once:** a numbered list, each item with a bold label and one question that can be answered alone, then a line saying why he asks.

```text
To help me plan, could you clarify two details?

1. **Timeline:** What is the target window for the work (3, 5, or 7 days)?
2. **Schedule:** Should I stay inside the team's working hours, or is the weekend fine?

For context, I'm blocking one or two days for this, so the answers decide which ones.
```

**Slack, working with a team:**

| Move | He writes |
|------|-----------|
| Take ownership | "I'm taking point on our side." |
| Commit to an update | "I'll post an update here before the end of the day." · "I'll keep everyone posted here." |
| Ask for time | "Do you have 5 minutes to sync?" |
| Invite input | "I would love to hear your thoughts and discuss next steps." · "Happy to discuss anything here in the thread!" |
| Leave the door open | "If anything changes on your side, feel free to ping me here!" |
| Restate a client's ask | "We understand you would like [the ask] ahead of [their milestone]." |
| Recommend | "Our recommendation: [option], because [reason]." |
| Ask before going back to the client | "Before I take this to [who], I'd like your input on [what]." |
| Keep one voice | "If anyone at [client] raises this with you directly, please point them to me, and let's not commit to dates or scope until we agree internally." |
| Show an assumption | "I read it as [the reading]. The rationale for this is:" · "So I don't promise a date here." |

## Spanish: customers, partners, university

Rioplatense, voseo, "ustedes" for groups. Friendly and direct, never stiff, never salesy.

| Slot | He writes |
|------|-----------|
| Greeting, warm | `Hola <Nombre>!` · `Buenas <Nombre>!` · `Buen día!` |
| Greeting, quick ack | `<Nombre>,` and straight to the point |
| Greeting, proposal or first formal mail | `Hola <Nombre>, <Nombre>, ¿cómo están?` |
| Greeting, administrative | `Estimados,` |
| Ack | "Recibido." · "Impecable!" · "Confirmo la reu." |
| Picking up a thread | "Tal como conversamos en la reunión, les comparto ..." |
| Introducing contents | "En el documento van a encontrar:" and a numbered list |
| Saying no | "No tenemos eso desarrollado actualmente. Podríamos realizarlo, pero requeriría ..." |
| Giving the reason | "La razón es que ..." · "Por este motivo, ..." |
| Opening a path | "Si les interesa sumarlos, podemos armar una propuesta más integral ..." |
| Low-pressure follow-up | "¿Pudieron ver la propuesta? Si quieren, agendamos un rato para charlar y sacarse cualquier duda, sin ningún tipo de compromiso." |
| After a no-show | "Veo que no te pudiste sumar a la llamada. Si te interesa charlar, podemos hablar por WhatsApp." |
| Logistics | Repeats the day, the time, and the link in the body, even when a calendar invite went out: "Dejo la info también acá:" |
| Close | "Quedo atento a cualquier duda." · "Quedo a disposición." · "Quedo atento a sus comentarios." |
| Thanks | "Gracias desde ya," · "Muchas gracias," |
| Sign-off | `Saludos,` · `Un saludo,` · `Nos vemos en un rato,` then `Tomás.` by default, `Tomi.` with people who already call him that. Name and role only on a first formal contact or a proposal |

**One person or the firm:** he writes to the person ("Si te interesa...") and switches to "ustedes" when the decision belongs to the firm ("Si les interesa sumarlos...").

**Vocabulary that is his:** "charlar", "armar", "sumar" and "sumarse", "agendar", "coordinar", "avanzar", "acá", "un rato", "sin problema", "cualquier duda". Future with "ir a": "van a encontrar", "vamos a dictar", "vas a poder".

**Self-introduction:** name, role, what the reader gets. "Buenas! Mi nombre es Tomás y soy cofundador de [producto]. Acá vas a poder agendar una reunión conmigo para charlar sobre tu estudio y entender si podemos ayudarte."

**Commitments carry a date:** "nos comprometemos a incluir [X] dentro de los siguientes 3 meses". What exists today and what is promised are in separate sentences.

**Emphasis in outreach:** in a rich-text email he sometimes bolds the two or three phrases that carry the offer. Leave it to him: a draft is plain text.

**Marks:** exclamations with the closing mark only ("Buenas!", "Gracias!"). Questions with both marks ("¿cómo están?"). He often drops the opening mark when he types fast. A draft in his voice keeps it, except in chat.

## Spanish: product updates

The warmest register. One person on the team writing to the people who use the product, as colleagues.

| Move | Example |
|------|---------|
| Greeting and a human line | "Buenas! Espero que hayan disfrutado el feriado largo y descansado como se merecen." |
| A scene from the reader's day | "En el día a día, manejás 20 pestañas y [producto] se mezcla entre tantas. O estás tomando un café y querés chequear un saldo mientras estás fuera del estudio." |
| Pick up a promise | "Hace un par de ediciones les contábamos que estábamos preparando la cancha para [X]. Bueno, ya están acá!" |
| What changed, what you gain | "Ahora podés [hacer X]. En vez de [lo que hacías antes], [lo que ves ahora]." |
| Reassure | "Podés seguir usándola como la usás hoy, no cambia nada!" |
| A fix | "Corregimos un error que por momentos hacía fallar [qué]. Si alguna vez te topaste con [el síntoma], eso ya quedó atrás." |
| A beta | "Esta primera versión sale en beta: queremos que la usen y nos cuenten qué les sirve y qué les falta." |
| Ask for feedback | "Si la probás, tu comentario nos viene bárbaro!" |
| Give them a line to reuse | "Si alguna vez te preguntaron «y cómo manejan la seguridad?», ahí tenés la respuesta para mostrar." |
| Thank the people who asked | "Si estás leyendo esto y alguno de estos pedidos te suena, gracias!" |
| Sign-off | "Un saludo," and the founders' first names |
| PS | One human line about the week. He has thanked a football player there |

**Colloquial words he uses here:** "la compu", "el celu", "el finde", "a un toque de distancia", "nos viene bárbaro", "sin vueltas", "hacían ruido", "preparando la cancha".

**Preview lines** are a fragment with the benefit: "A un toque de distancia, a pantalla completa y más rápida."

He sometimes doubles an exclamation mark in a PS. Leave his when you edit his text, and don't add one of your own.

## Posts and comments

From the LinkedIn posts he wrote by hand, in Spanish and in English. He writes from something that happened, in the first person, warm and modest, and he credits other people by name.

| Move | Spanish | English |
|------|---------|---------|
| Open with the news | "Tengo una gran noticia" · "Confirmado [evento] en Argentina!" | "First place in [hackathon]!" |
| Open with the topic | "Privacidad on-chain." · "Tecnología > Precios" | "Why build a CLI tool instead of an IDE?" |
| Say how he feels about it | "Me hace feliz compartirles que..." · "Es un gusto para mí..." · "Estas oportunidades me encantan." | "What a blast of a weekend." |
| Opinion marker | "Para mí, ..." · "Y ojo, ..." | "My take: ..." · "In my opinion, ..." |
| Thank by name | "Gracias [nombre] por la invitación!" · "gracias por bancar" | "Kudos to [name] for..." · "Thanks for sharing" |
| Praise, specific | "Vengan a escuchar a [nombre], que sabe mucho y explica excelente." · "Gran laburo" · "la rompieron" | "Great read!" · "Rocking it" |
| Invite | "Los invito a..." · "Sumate" · "Si te interesa saber más, ..." | "Who's in for next year?" |
| Say who it is for | "Si trabajás en [sector] y querés entender [tema], este programa es para vos." | |
| Point to the link | "Dejo el link en el primer comentario." | "Link in the first comment." |
| Close | "Vamos por más" · "A seguir aprendiendo" · "Los esperamos!" · "Se viene con todo" | "Let's keep building" · "Let's go!" · "What do you think?" |

**His words here:** "tremendo", "lindo", "hermoso", "excelente", "un gusto", "charlar", "arrancamos", "se viene", "golazo", "laburo". In English: "amazing", "awesome", "for sure", "bullish", and "&" for "and".

A recap with thanks, with the names taken out:

```text
Tecnología > Precios

Los invito a revivir el stream donde recorremos los orígenes de la tecnología blockchain y lo que significa una "cadena de bloques" 🚀

Gracias [nombre] por la invitación!

Hermoso estudio, y un gran agradecimiento por generar este tipo de espacios para charlar sobre estos temas.

Dejo el link en el primer comentario 🦾
```

An opinion post in English:

```text
Why build a CLI tool instead of an IDE? It's an interesting product question.

[Company] launched its new coding assistant today, and it is a CLI.

It made me think of something [name] said about why building CLI tools can be smarter than building full IDEs.

Is the future going to be more about natural language and CLI-powered actions than about UIs & buttons?

What do you think?
```

**Comments** are a line: "Grande [nombre]! Éxitos!" · "Tremendo laburo!" · "Coincido, el ecosistema es mucho más grande que esto!" · "Completely agree. Let's go!" · "Great image! Do you have the source?"

## Spanish: teaching and talks

He teaches executives and gives talks. The stance is sober: the technology in use, not in theory.

| Move | Example |
|------|---------|
| Promise first | "Hoy vamos a aprender a distinguir el trigo de la paja." |
| Meet the room where it is | "Seguramente hayan escuchado hablar de ... Y esto es normal: la especulación vende más que hablar de tecnología." |
| Say what is out and what is in | A short list of "No voy a hablar de ..." followed by "Vamos a entender ...", "Vamos a analizar casos reales ..." |
| Roadmap in three | "Y lo vamos a hacer en tres secciones:" |
| Everyday analogy | "Una Ferrari para ir al kiosco." · "Pensalo como una ficha, pero digital y programable." |
| Start from what they know | "Todos entendemos qué es escanear un documento, ¿no?" |
| A test they can run | "¿Podés resolverlo con una base de datos? Entonces no lo necesitás." |
| Name the limit | "No es para todo (y está perfecto)." · "virtudes y falencias" |
| Evidence over promise | "casos reales", "en uso, no en teoría", "separar entusiasmo de utilidad concreta" |

Spoken sentences are short. He addresses the room as "ustedes" and a slide or a single reader as "vos". A question to the room is a real question, so it stays.

**What he keeps out of a talk:** prices, investment advice, and "revolución". Saying so at the start is part of the voice.

## Spanish: product copy

For a B2B product whose users are busy professionals. The copy shows their own day back to them.

| Move | Example |
|------|---------|
| Start with a verb, first person plural | "Sincronizamos los datos de toda tu cartera todos los días, así dejás de descargarlos cliente por cliente." · "Te avisamos cuando cambia el domicilio de un cliente, antes de que se entere él." |
| Their moment, in numbers | "Cliente 1 de 47." · "2 horas y media. Todos los lunes." · "Tu lunes son 3 clientes, no 47." |
| Open with their objection and honor it | "¿Le doy mis claves a un software?" then "Es la pregunta correcta. Te muestro cómo funciona." |
| Show the old way, don't name an enemy | Side by side, same task, both running. No "X is dead" |
| Let them do the math | "Hacé la cuenta conmigo." · "Con tus números, no los míos." |
| Conservative claims | Understate the hours saved. Conservative converts better in B2B |
| One thing per piece | One screen, one use case, one promise. A second feature gets cut |
| Call to action | Imperative in voseo plus the frictions removed: "Probalo 15 días. Sin tarjeta." |
| Understatement | Show the bad outcome plainly and leave it. No dramatic music, no extra line |

Each claim about what the product does must be true exactly as worded. One loose promise drops the credibility of the whole piece.

**Why verbs:** the brain understands verbs. An abstract noun ("plataforma", "ecosistema", "solución") makes the other person guess what you do, and nobody buys what they have to guess. In his words, it "suena a SaaS genérico" and the buyer "detecta el humo".

**On a sales call** he says how the call will go and that "no" is fine: "Te hago un par de preguntas, te muestro solo lo que te sirve, y al final puede ser sí, no, o seguir charlando. No te vengo a apurar."

## English: feedback and plans

Long-form, written for another team or a manager.

| Move | Example |
|------|---------|
| Thanks first, and the frame | "Above all, I want to thank the team for the support throughout the process. Please take what follows as friendly user feedback." |
| Explain the structure in one line | "I have organized the feedback by [category]. Each item has a description and a screenshot where relevant." |
| Tagged items | `[Bug]`, `[Improvement]`, `[Feedback]` and a plain title that states the problem |
| Item body | What happened, why it mattered to us, a small suggestion, the evidence |
| Calibrate | "Completely acceptable, and I wouldn't expect it to be heavily optimized, but it's an improvement opportunity." |
| Give the setup | Machine, browser, connection speed, date. Whatever lets them reproduce it |
| Credit | "In both cases, the team quickly clarified and resolved the discrepancy." |
| One dry line | "This may be one of those famous cases of 'it's not a bug, it's a feature', so dismiss it if so." |
| Close | "Thank you for reading through all of this!" |

**Plans and scenario write-ups:** assumptions listed first and labeled as assumptions, roles and boundaries ("I own ...", "[Lead] owns the decision on ..."), then a timeline of actions. Each action is a bold verb phrase and a colon, then one or two sentences. Messages he would send are drafted in full, ready to paste. In a scenario or a template, roles go in brackets: `[Lead]`, `[BD]`.

## English: his own site and bio

First person, short, led by verbs and numbers.

| Move | Pattern |
|------|---------|
| Headline | "I design, build, and ship [kind of] products." Three real actions, in the order they happen |
| Proof | "Built and shipped [product] end-to-end, now used daily by [N] firms." |
| Call to action | "Got something hard to build? Tell me about it." |
| Label | A role and two or three fields, separated by a middle dot |
| Legal and privacy text | Plain statements of fact with no exclamation marks: "The site sets no cookies and runs no analytics." Every sentence must be true of the site as built |

## How he does not sound

- **Not corporate.** No "Espero que se encuentren muy bien", no "Please do not hesitate to contact me", no "Reciba un cordial saludo".
- **Not a copywriter.** No sensory adjectives for effect ("silky-smooth"), no cliffhangers, no "stop the scroll" hooks.
- **Not a guru.** No lessons learned in numbered commandments, no humble-brag opener, no "Here's what nobody tells you", no empty motivational tone.
- **Not neutral Spanish.** No "tú", no "ordenador", no "¡Descubre cómo!".
- **Not breathless.** No urgency, no "don't miss out", no stacked exclamation marks.
- **Not absolute.** He rarely says "always", "never", "the best", or "guaranteed" about a product or a technology.
