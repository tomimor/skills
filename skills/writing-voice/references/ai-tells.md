# AI tells

The patterns that make a reader think "a model wrote this", in English and Spanish, each with a rewrite. Structure gives a draft away more than vocabulary does, so the structural tells come first.

The test behind every entry is Tomi's own: **if a sentence gives the reader no information and no concrete value, delete it.** The text should sound like one person on the team writing to a colleague.

## Structural tells

| Tell | Looks like | Rewrite |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Contrast drumbeat | "It isn't a tool. It's a mindset." · "No es solo software. Es tranquilidad." | State the second half with its evidence: "Te avisa el mismo día que cambia el domicilio de un cliente." |
| Forced triad | "Simple. Fast. Reliable." · "Rápido, seguro y confiable." | The one that matters, with its number: "Sincroniza 47 clientes antes de las 7." |
| Stacked fragments | "No meetings. No noise. Just results." | A full sentence |
| A question asked only so you can answer it | "The result? A 40% lift." · "¿El resultado? Menos trabajo." | "Conversion went up 40%." |
| Engagement bait | "Agree?" · "Thoughts?" · "¿Qué opinan?" tacked on the end | End on the takeaway. Ask only what he wants answered |
| Every item on one template | Each bullet is "**Bold label:** benefit sentence." of equal length | Vary by content. Some items are one line, one deserves three |
| Parallel openings | Three paragraphs in a row starting "Whether you're...", "When you...", "Si sos..." | Start each from its own fact |
| Echo ending | A last line that restates the piece as an aphorism | End on the next step or the last fact |
| Setup then reveal | "Here's the thing:" · "Acá viene lo interesante:" | Say the thing |
| False range | "From startups to enterprises" · "desde monotributistas hasta grandes empresas" | Name the actual reader |
| Summary of what was just said | "In short, ..." after four sentences | Cut it |

## Openers and closers

| Tell | Rewrite |
|------|---------|
| "I hope this email finds you well." · "Espero que te encuentres muy bien." | Go straight in, or one real line: "Espero que hayan disfrutado el feriado largo." |
| "In today's fast-moving world..." · "En el mundo de hoy..." | Start with the answer |
| "We're excited to announce..." · "Nos enorgullece anunciar..." · "Estamos emocionados..." | Say what shipped and what the reader gains |
| "I wanted to reach out to..." | The ask |
| "Let me know if you have any questions or if there's anything else I can help with." | His real close: "Quedo atento a cualquier duda." or a concrete next step |
| "We hope you enjoy these improvements." · "Esperamos que disfrutes estas mejoras." | Thank the people who asked for them, or stop |
| "And much more." · "Y mucho más." | Name the next thing, or stop |
| "We keep improving week after week." · "Seguimos mejorando semana a semana." | Delete |

## Vocabulary

Replace each with the measurable thing it stands for. If there is nothing measurable behind the word, the sentence goes.

| Family | English | Spanish |
|--------|---------|---------|
| Hype adjectives | `revolutionary`, `game-changing`, `cutting-edge`, `seamless`, `robust`, `powerful` | `revolucionario`, `disruptivo`, `potente`, `increíble`, `de vanguardia` |
| Empty verbs | `leverage`, `unlock`, `harness`, `elevate`, `empower`, `supercharge` | `potenciar`, `empoderar`, `llevar al siguiente nivel` |
| Category nouns | `platform`, `solution`, `ecosystem` as the subject of a pitch | `plataforma integral`, `solución de gestión`, `ecosistema para...` |
| Signposts | `it's worth noting`, `it's important to note`, `needless to say` | `cabe destacar`, `es importante mencionar`, `vale la pena destacar` |
| Stock metaphors | `landscape`, `journey`, `tapestry`, `deep dive`, `delve` | `cambio de paradigma`, `un antes y un después` |
| Intensifiers | `really`, `extremely`, `incredibly`, `truly` | `súper`, `sumamente`, `increíblemente` |

**Category nouns get verbs instead.** A pitch line starts with what the product does, in the first person plural: "Sincronizamos [la fuente] todos los días para toda tu cartera, así dejás de descargar datos cliente por cliente." If a line starts with "Somos una plataforma de...", rewrite it.

**Builder jargon stays out of text for customers:** internal field names, backticks, ticket numbers, "X/Y", English tech terms the reader doesn't use. The reader's own professional acronyms go in unexplained. They are part of the reader's language.

## Tone

| Tell | Rewrite |
|------|---------|
| Hedge stack: "You might want to consider perhaps..." | "I'd do X, because Y." |
| Sycophancy: "Great question!" · "¡Excelente pregunta!" | Answer it |
| Praise sandwich in feedback | The concern, stated once and specifically. Credit separately where it is true |
| Scripted empathy: "I understand how you feel." · "Entiendo cómo te sentís." | Address the concern itself |
| Manufactured urgency: "Don't miss out." · "Últimos lugares." | The real date, if there is one |
| Pressure close: "Can we get this signed today?" | "Al final puede ser sí, no, o seguir charlando." |
| Empty motivation: "Keep building." · "El futuro es de los que se animan." | A fact the reader can use |
| A label where a number would do: "a big client", "cliente grande" | The number: "90 cuentas". The reader judges |
| Overpromise: "always", "guaranteed", "the best" | What it does, and where it stops |

## Formatting

| Tell | Rewrite |
|------|---------|
| Em dash, anywhere | A comma for a short pause, a colon for an explanation, parentheses for a longer aside, a period to separate ideas. This is his one non-negotiable rule |
| Bold on every line | One or two emphases per item at most. If everything is highlighted, nothing stands out |
| Emoji at the start of every bullet or the end of every sentence | None, or the fixed set of section markers the piece already uses |
| Headings in an email or chat message | Paragraphs |
| A bulleted list for what is one sentence | The sentence |
| ALL CAPS for emphasis | Word choice |
| Hashtags, in the body or as a closing block | None |
| A translated quote or title | The original, with a short comment in his words. A translated quote reads as "medio mala" |
| Decoration that carries no information (an accent bar, a mascot, an icon per card) | Remove it. "Es muy de AI" |

## What is not a tell

These are his, so leave them alone.

- **"Buenas!" and "Gracias!" with a closing mark only.** That is how he writes exclamations in Spanish, in email and in product updates. An exclamation on a greeting, thanks, or good news is his. One on a claim is not.
- **Argentine colloquial phrases** in customer-facing Spanish: "sin vueltas", "nos viene bárbaro", "preparando la cancha", "la compu", "el finde". They set him apart. Keep them, as long as they don't overpromise.
- **One concrete contrast:** "Tu lunes son 3 clientes, no 47."
- **A real question to a real audience,** in a talk or in a call script.
- **A test question the reader answers about their own case:** "¿Podés resolverlo con una base de datos? Entonces no lo necesitás." The reader supplies the answer, so it is a tool and not a setup.
- **A parenthetical aside,** one per paragraph.
- **A short PS** with something human in a product update.
- **Three verbs that are three real actions:** "I design, build, and ship products." The tell is the forced triad, where the third item is there for the rhythm.
- **"Quedo atento a cualquier duda."** It reads like a stock close, and it is his.

## What the scan covers

`scripts/voice-scan.sh` flags candidates for: `emdash`, `hype`, `filler`, `hedge`, `contrast`, `hook`, `intensifier`, `exclaim`, `hashtag`, `emoji`, and `placeholder` (a `[Missing: ...]` or `[Completar: ...]` left in the text).

It can't see the structural tells that need judgment: a triad, items on one template, an echo ending, a buried answer, a missing next step, an invented fact. Read for those yourself, in that order of cost: invented fact first, buried answer second.
