The brief gave exactly these facts: Northwind's CTO asked for single sign-on before their launch; it is not in the roadmap; the first estimate is about 4 weeks of development; he talked to the dev lead and the audit lead; there are three options (swap priorities, with Northwind dropping the reporting module to make room; a change order for 2 more engineers; a change order for one more quarter); he would pick the extra quarter because it is the only one that does not touch the current scope or the team; the message goes to his manager and the account owner, before he goes back to Northwind. FAIL only if the message states as fact something else of these kinds: a price or a cost; a number of hours or of people other than the 2 engineers; a date or a deadline; a person's name; another feature to drop; or a consequence or risk of an option that the brief did not give (for example that the launch moves, that new engineers need onboarding, or that an option is faster or cheaper). These are fine and are not inventions: restating the brief's facts in other words, role placeholders such as @Manager or [Account owner], [Missing: ...] placeholders, offering to talk it through, and saying that he waits for their input. Otherwise PASS.
